# Lesson 11 - Assembly quality control

!!! hint "Learning objectives"

    - Explain the meaning of *de novo* assembly quality control metrics, including N50 and L50
    - Assess the quality of a *de novo* bacterial genome assembly

## 11.1 Basics of assembly QC

### Talking points

- Methods for assessing a *de novo* assembly's quality
    - Summary statistics
        - N50: the **length** of the shortest contig that, along with all other contigs of equal or longer length, make up 50% of the total assembly length.
            - 50% of the total assembly is contained in contigs of equal or greater length than N50.
            - Larger N50 = longer, more complete contigs; smaller N50 = smaller, more fragmented contigs.
        - L50: the smallest **number** of contigs whose length makes up 50% of the total assembly length.
            - Smaller L50 = longer, more complete contigs; larger L50 = smaller, more fragmented contigs.
        - Similar metrics, e.g. N90 mean the same thing but for a different percentage of the assembly.
            - N90 = 90% of the total assembly is contained in contigs of equal or greater length.
    - BUSCO - Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologues.
        - A tool for estimating the completeness of a genome assembly by looking for highly-conserved genes.
        - Universal single-copy orthologues = genes that are highly conserved across kingdoms of organisms and for which only a single copy is normally present.
            - Presence/absence, completeness/fragmentedness, and copy number status can be used to estimate assembly quality.
            - Low BUSCO score is problematic; however, high BUSCO score *does not necessarily* mean the assembly is of high quality, as it only takes into account a small proportion of the total assembly.
    - For bacterial genomics, contig circularisation is also an important metric
        - Most bacteria have a single, circular chromosome and possibly several smaller, also circular plasmids.
        - Linear sequences in a species not expected to have them suggests incomplete assembly, e.g. due to low coverage
        - Inspecting the assembly graph can help to identify poor-quality bacterial assemblies
            - One large circular contig + possible smaller circular contigs = good
            - Many small, linear fragments = bad
            - Highly-branching assembly graph = bad - coverage or quality of sequencing too low, leading to uncertainty in how the genome is structured

## 11.2 Tools for assessing *de novo* assembly quality

### Talking points

- QUAST - generates N50/L50 metrics, as well as assembly length, number of contigs, etc.
- BUSCO - generates BUSCO scores, notes # of complete & single-copy BUSCOs, fragmented BUSCOs, missing BUSCOs, and duplicated BUSCOs.
- Bandage - visualisation tool for inspecting assembly graphs, such as those generated by flye

## 11.3 Exercises

### Tasks

- Run QUAST on your assemblies
    - What was the N50 and L50 for your assemblies? Does this indicate a good or bad quality assembly?
    - What was your longest contig?
    - What was the total assembly length?
- Run BUSCO on your assemblies
    - How many complete & single-copy BUSCOs were found?
    - How many BUSCOs were duplicated?
    - How many BUSCOs were fragmented or missing?
    - How would you interpret this result? Was it a good assembly or bad?
- Run Bandage on your assemblies
    - Describe your assembly.
        - Did you find mostly circular or linear contigs?
        - Did you find a single large chromosome?
        - Did you find many small plasmids?
