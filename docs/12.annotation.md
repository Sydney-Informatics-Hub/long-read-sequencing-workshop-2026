# Lesson 12 - Genome annotation and AMR gene detection

!!! hint "Learning objectives"

    - Explain the need for genome annotation for *de novo* assemblies
    - Use tools like `bakta` and `AMRFinderPlus` and public databases to annotate an assembled genome with features such as antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes

## 12.1 Goals for bacterial genome annotations

### Talking points

- At this point, we have a polished bacterial genome assembly constructed from long reads
    - No longer dealing with long read-specific issues - now onto the wide expanse of options for downstream analysis
    - We will finish the workshop looking at a common use-case for bacterial long read sequencing and genome assembly - antimicrobial resistance (AMR) gene detection.
- AMR genes can be present either on the bacterial chromosome or in plasmids
    - Plasmids are mobile, can be passed between organisms and even species
    - AMR genes on plasmids are therefore highly important for the spread of AMR genes, e.g. in hospital settings
- Lots of AMR genes already known and documented - databases exist for annotation purposes
- Our workshop context: outbreak of hospital-acquired, antimicrobial resistant infections
    - Resistant to 'carbapenem' drug class - a 'last resort' antibiotic class that is very effective, but is reserved for the most severe, multiple drug-resistant cases.
        - Members of the 'beta-lactam (bla) antibiotic drug class.
        - Most clinically significant genes:
            - blaKPC: *Klebsiella pneumoniae* carbapenemase
            - blaIMP: Imipenemase-type metallo-β-lactamase
            - blaVIM: Verona integron-encoded metallo-β-lactamase
            - blaNDM: New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase
            - blaOXA: Oxacillinase-48

## 12.2 Tools for bacterial genome annotation

### Talking points

- For AMR gene detection:
    - AMRFinderPlus - A tool from NCBI to identify known AMR genes in nucleotide and/or protein sequences, as well as known resistance-associated SNPs.
    - Abricate - Detects AMR as well as virulence genes that influence the infectious nature of a species. Draws from multiple databases.
- For more general bacterial genome annotation: bakta
    - Bundles many tools and databases to annotate bacterial genomes for a wide range of features, including:
        - RNA genes, including tRNA, rRNA, ncRNA, and CRISPR arrays
        - Coding sequences and protein-coding genes
        - Also wraps AMRFinderPlus for AMR gene detection
